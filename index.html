Signal

How the encoding works

Your text is converted to bytes, wrapped with a length header and a checksum, then split into 4-bit chunks. Each chunk becomes one of 16 tones, evenly spaced and confined to less than one octave — this trades some musicality for a guarantee that no tone's natural harmonic distortion can be mistaken for a different tone. A single high marker tone signals "start"; "end" is a dual-tone chord (low + high together) so a lone low frequency in the room can't fake it. If your message starts with a known shortened-URL prefix (like is.gd), that prefix is replaced with a single flag byte instead of being transmitted character-by-character. Repeats give listeners in a noisy room more chances to catch a clean pass.
