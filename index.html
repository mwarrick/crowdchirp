How the encoding works

Your text is converted to bytes, wrapped with a length header and a checksum, then split into 4-bit chunks. Each chunk becomes one of 16 tones drawn from a musical scale (so sequences land as a little melody, not random beeps). A high marker tone signals "start", a different high tone signals "end" — kept well clear of typical phone-mic low-frequency roll-off. Repeats give listeners in a noisy room more chances to catch a clean pass.
